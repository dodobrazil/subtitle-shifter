How to convert SRT to TXT

  1. Choose your .srt file, or paste the subtitle text into the box.
  2. Pick One line per cue (each subtitle on its own line) or Paragraphs (running text split at pauses).
  3. Press Download .txt. The file keeps the original name with a .txt extension.

An SRT file is plain text already, but every subtitle is wrapped in a counter and a time range such as 00:00:01,000 --> 00:00:03,500. This tool removes that structure and joins two-line subtitles back into single lines, which is what you want for transcripts, translation, study notes or pasting into a document.

FAQ

How do I convert SRT to TXT?

Choose your .srt file or paste its text and press Download. The tool removes the cue numbers and timestamps and keeps only the spoken text. Nothing is uploaded.

Can I keep the timestamps in the text file?

Yes. Tick Add [hh:mm:ss] timestamps to put the start time of each cue in front of its line. This works in One line per cue layout.

How do I get readable paragraphs instead of one line per subtitle?

Choose Paragraphs. Cues are joined into running text and a new paragraph starts whenever there is a pause longer than the gap you set (2 seconds by default).

What happens to italic tags and formatting codes?

HTML-style tags such as <i> and <c.yellow> and ASS codes such as {\an8} are removed by default, so you get clean text. Untick Strip tags to keep them.

Does it work with VTT, ASS and YouTube subtitles?

Yes. WebVTT and ASS/SSA files are detected automatically. Auto-generated captions often repeat the same line in consecutive cues; Remove repeated lines drops those duplicates.

Are my files uploaded?

No. The extraction runs in your browser with JavaScript and your file never leaves your device.
