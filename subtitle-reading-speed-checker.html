How reading speed is checked

  1. Load the subtitle file or paste it, and set your limits.
  2. Press Check. Each cue gets characters per second (CPS), its longest line (CPL) and its duration.
  3. Fix flagged cues in your editor, or download the CSV to work through them in a spreadsheet.

Example: a 1.0 second cue with 18 visible characters reads at 18 CPS, which is over a 17 CPS limit, so it is flagged. Either lengthen the cue or shorten the text.

Formatting tags are ignored, spaces and punctuation count, line breaks do not.

FAQ

What is CPS in subtitles?

CPS means characters per second: the number of visible characters in a cue divided by how long it is on screen. It is the usual measure of how fast a viewer has to read.

What are good CPS, CPL and duration limits?

The defaults here (17 CPS, 42 characters per line, 0.833 s minimum and 7 s maximum duration) are common guideline values, but limits differ by client, language and audience. Every limit is editable, so enter the numbers from your own style guide.

How are characters counted?

Formatting tags such as italic tags and ASS override codes are ignored, spaces and punctuation count, and line breaks do not count. Line length (CPL) is the longest single line in the cue.

Can I export the results?

Yes. Download CSV gives one row per cue with timing, characters, CPS, line length and the issues found, ready for a spreadsheet.

Is my file uploaded anywhere?

No. The check runs in your browser and the file never leaves your device.

Which formats work?

SRT, WebVTT and ASS/SSA can be loaded.
